### _[2026: New Venue #34](2026newvenuelist)_

You may have heard of Quinnipiac University as a school known for its polling, but Quinnipiac also has a very interesting (men's) hockey team: similar to fellow Connecticut schools UConn and [Sacred Heart](2025-12-31_sacredhearthockey_mens), they were a lower level program before moving to D-I, and were in what is now Atlantic Hockey America before moving to the ECAC in the early 2000s. Since 2013, they’ve largely dominated the ECAC: they've won its regular season title ten times and made the NCAA tournament in each of those seasons (including every year post-COVID), plus they won a national championship in 2023 and have two other championship game appearances in that timespan[^1] (don’t ask about their number of official conference titles however). Because they’ve become such a powerhouse in the ECAC, some have advocated for them to jump conferences again and join the much higher profile Hockey East (i.e. the conference I saw a bunch of times back in 2023); I don’t know how the school feels about the prospects, but I’m not sure I can see it happening anytime soon. I also found in researching them that their list of alumni in the NHL is kinda light; case in point: they have more alums who have played less than ten NHL games (six) than those who played over 100 (three).

Similar to Sacred Heart just down the highway (which is technically NOT the Merritt Parkway - at least not until you cross the Housatonic River), their arena is not on the university's main campus, but a little bit west of it. Unlike Sacred Heart, however, it’s not within a secondary campus; from what I could tell, the arena is just surrounded by dorms and a student center. It was opened in 2007 after the hockey team spent quite some time at a nearby community rink (WELL off campus), and similar to [Holy Cross](2025-10-21_holycrosshockey_mens) (and, to certain extents, [Merrimack](2025-10-10_merrimackhockey_mens)), it has fully separate facilities for hockey and basketball adjacent to one another (which share a singular common lobby)[^2]; unlike those places however, both facilities are actually fairly "equal" - to the point where the arenas themselves are nearly identical.

![An image of the hockey setup at M&T Bank Arena](/images/2026/1121-hockey.jpeg)

The two arenas share a common lobby which has a concession stand and a little viewing area (the campus the arena is in is on top of a hill), and the design of each arena is fairly simple: large concrete walls with various banners, a fairly large set of windows and one-way doors near the lobby, and most of the seats are in a singular bowl (with premium seats above the entrance). Alcohol is not served in the event space, but rather in the arena which is NOT in use at the given time (so for this hockey game, it was in the basketball arena). I took advantage of this to see what , and the biggest difference between the two seems to be that the basketball arena has retractable seats to set up extra baskets for practice - like this:

Given Quinnipiac's status as a college hockey power, it's only natural that someone like me who would want to check them out. However, they're two hours away from both where I am currently AND Boston, so the logistics of trying to go to a game are always tricky (unless I REALLY want to be driving up/down the Merritt Parkway late at night). Thankfully, there are several "midday-ish" games on their schedule (4 PM), so even though "it does get late early out there" during hockey season (i.e. winter), it's not TOO bad (just be wary of towns with speed cameras...). Given that the entire lower bowl was one uniform price ($30 - which isn't too bad for a top team like Quinnipiac), I went back and forth on which seat I wanted before settling on...

![An image of the hockey arena at M&T Bank Arena from the front row](/images/2026/1121-frontrow-quinnipiac.jpeg)

<figcaption>I must admit it was fun to watch the game here, but I kinda like the view from the top better...</figcaption>

This game was a bit of a wild one, with Quinnipiac putting NINE goals past the [College of the Holy Cross](2025-10-21_holycrosshockey_mens)[^3] and both teams using ALL THREE of their goalies (college hockey teams have three goalies and I THINK an extra skater during games, plus I found out at this game that they added the NHL trapezoid this year). What I found somewhat unfortunate, however, was a distinct lack of student presence: there was a very small band and some cheerleaders, but it felt like they were the only students around[^4]. Also, I have no idea why, but this game was moved UP to 3:30 from an originally scheduled start of 4 PM, which actually made it more convenient for me as it meant I could start heading home slightly earlier.

One other note: Quinnipiac was one of four places I considered going to on Halloween - mainly as I would have seen TWO GAMES (the first one being a TOP TEN matchup against Providence, and the second one featuring the also top ten ranked women's team[^5]) - but I ended up deciding against it due to timing and distance (I would have had to have left home at 9 AM for a noontime start on the day before the return to Standard Time) plus other factors surrounding the [New York City Marathon](2026-11-01_nycmarathon).

### MERCH

Much like Sacred Heart, my first visit to the school was a quick stop at the campus bookstore to check out if they had jerseys (I actually visited the two bookstores on the same drive). Unlike my initial Sacred Heart visit, there were jerseys in the bookstore (though they were all "fan" jerseys) and parking was easier to find (though the bookstore wasn't in many respects).

At this game, merchandise was in one of the corners. While there wasn't much that really caught my attention, I should note that there were a few sublimated replicas of the team's (former) Adidas jerseys, but...I felt they were a bit too much for what they are. Also, warmup pucks here have a design on them (which is something I haven't seen a lot of in college hockey); one of the players tossed one my way, but between it being a bit worn and me not knowing if I actually wanted to bring it home or not, I ended up giving it to some kids who were trying to get a puck after warmups (after I took this photo).

![An image of a warmup puck along the glass](/images/2026/1121-warmuppuck.jpeg)

As an aside, I've seen "authentic" jerseys for both Quinnipiac and Sacred Heart available on eBay (as well as many other schools whose jerseys I haven't seen sold for that reason), but while they're not as much as NHL jerseys, they're on the "a bit out of my comfort price" range plus I don't know how authentic they are (though college hockey doesn't really feel like a sport worth counterfeiting...).
