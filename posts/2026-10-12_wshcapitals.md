### _[2026: New Venue #30](2026newvenuelist)_

Prior to winning the Stanley Cup in 2018, the Washington Capitals were probably best known for two things:

1. The worst regular season in NHL history (their first season in 1974-75, in which they had a winning percentage of exactly .100)

2. Their inability to go deep into the playoffs despite having Alexander Ovechkin - one of the NHL's top talents of the 2000s (I mean check out <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E82oUXP6Hng" target="blank">this goal from his first season in the NHL</a>). As an aside, this post will NOT be getting into some of the "less savory" details about him (i.e. when you combine his country of origin with certain leanings common amongst hockey players...).

Since winning that Cup, they've won exactly ONE playoff series - though Ovechkin did what was once thought to be impossible and claim the title of the NHL's all time leading goalscorer (though he ain't getting the points record from Gretzky - partially for reasons I'll get into in a sec).

I'll be honest: I'm looking into going to a few NHL games this year (though I feel like that shouldn't come as a surprise). A lot of that is because I've only been to NHL games at THREE arenas, and I figured I'd like to check out a few more (mainly with the advent of this blog). On that note, however, I must admit that THIS particular game was not really on my "radar" until a week before it happened - despite me knowing for over a month that I'd be in town for the [Banana Bowl](2026-10-11_bananabowl2026). Thankfully, the Caps just so happened to be playing at home the day before (Friday) AND after (Sunday); I chose the "after" game for a few reasons:

1. I didn't want to have to rush down on a Friday afternoon to catch a 7:00 PM puck drop, and since I was staying with my brother, I wouldn't have to worry about spending an extra night at a hotel.

2. It was slightly less expensive when I got the tickets.

3. It featured a team I haven't seen yet: the Seattle Kraken. I have seen FAR fewer NHL teams vs. MLB (since, as mentioned earlier, I've only been to three arenas before this), but I figured trying to get to 32/32 (and then potentially more) would be fun to try (the "before" game would have been the [Rangers](2026-09-23_nyrangers), and despite that game being a preseason game, most of the team's NHL roster played)[^2]

On some level, I felt like I jumped early; I bought this ticket immediately after learning that the Caps were home when I was going to be in DC (which was about a week before the Banana Bowl), and I might have been able to find something a bit "better" had I waited a few days. What I was NOT expecting, however, was a fourth reason why going to this game was prudent:

4. Alex Ovechkin announced that this would be his final season about 24 hours after I bought the ticket - though it didn't seem to affect the market for this particular game in a major way (part of that may be the Commanders being home on the same day FWIW[^3])

The day before the game, I decided to check out the team's practice facility a short Metro ride away (for reasons I'll get to in a sec), and the team was practicing (it was open for fans to watch). One particular player wasn't present for the team practice, but I believe he was busy entertaining some young fans (who were probably his kids) in an adjacent rink...

That aside, what is now Capital One Arena opened in DC's Chinatown neighborhood in 1997 as a replacement for an arena known as the Capital Centre (though despite the name, it was in Maryland[^1]). If you remember me talking about the [Philadelphia arena saga](2026-04-12_phiwings2), then you might remember me mentioning that the Sixers wanted an arena in Philly's Chinatown, which was feared to be disruptive to the area - with DC as an example of what could happen (i.e. the reduction of Chinatown). I have no reference of how the neighborhood was prior to the arena's arrival, but at least according to Wikipedia, it has been cited as part of a "revitalization" of the neighborhood.

At this point, I probably don't need to talk about NBA/NHL arena layouts as they're all largely the same: main concourse for the lower bowl, upper concourse for the upper decks, both fully encircle the building to allow fans access to their seats. However, what makes THIS arena a bit unique (besides the fact that like Philly, the main concourse is at street level) is the fact that there's three levels of "non-suite" seats and a "middle" concourse for the second level - though a premium area means it doesn't go fully around the arena. While the concourses aren't exactly "special", my biggest gripe with them is the complete lack of natural lighting - though the building is currently undergoing a renovation (like other arenas built in the 1990s), so there's a chance this may not be the case in the future.

![A photo inside Capital One Arena](/images/2026/1011-c1arena.jpeg)

Inside the arena itself, there are FAR more video screens than most arenas I've seen: in addition to your standard center jumbotron, there are double-sided screens in each of the four corners. The ones facing the center of the arena display stats and stuff, while the ones facing out show the game and score to fans stuck immediately behind them.

If you're afraid of goal horns, be advised that much like MOST NHL buildings (save for the two Gardens and apparently Toronto), the arena has one that sounds after the team scores (though I probably should've mentioned this a [LONG time ago](2025-08-28_nysirens)). I believe most NHL teams have their goal horns near the center of the building (i.e. above or inside the jumbotron), so how far you are from it will determine how loud it sounds (it wasn't that loud in the ends, but a bit louder on the sides).

The bad news: the Capitals lost on a late goal (which happened with about five minutes to go in the game). The good news: I got to see Ovechkin score his first goal of this last season. One other thing I will say: the crowd was actually pretty into it (moreso than the Rangers game I felt) - even in the Metro station after the game.

### MERCH

I don't know if this is still a thing people do, but back in the day, one of the more "common" ways to get things without having to go to a store was via phone ordering (largely due to infomercials[^4]). Of course, a lot of this has been replaced with online shopping, but in 2023...I called the team's practice facility and placed a phone order for a Capitals jersey I REALLY wanted. It's part of an old program Adidas ran back in the day known as "Reverse Retro", and the Capitals apparently had one of the top selling jerseys of said program (hence why I had to literally call them - no one had it online).

Since I just mentioned the the Capitals' practice facility, I should mention that the team maintains a merchandise store here[^5]! While it does carry many of the same things the arena has (though it also had a magazine that I didn't see at the arena store), there weren't really many player jerseys save for a bunch of Ovechkins plus ONE Tom Wilson. What it DID have that I don't often see, however, were Authentic Pro Jerseys (i.e. the ones worn by the players on the ice).

As for the arena itself, I've TRIED to visit the store here quite a few times, but in my experiences, it's been closed more often than it's been open. I feel like I can only remember ONE visit to the store, but I was able to find a hat for the Arena Football team that played here back in the late 2010s that time[^5].

In the days leading up to this game, I found out that as part of the arena renovations I mentioned above, the store has moved to a new location. This location - similar to [TD Garden] (and, to certain extents, Madison Square Garden) - is outside of the arena itself, but you can visit it once you get into the arena by getting a handstamp from the ushers (the Devils actually have a fairly similar setup). Also, be forewarned that the merch areas inside the stadium are all VERY small and, for the most part, don't have anywhere near the selection of the main store.

Unlike the practice facility store, they had other player jerseys available, but with the exception of Ovechkin (and specifically RED Ovechkin jerseys as I thought about getting a white one), the jerseys featured heat pressed nameplates and numbers - including the one I actually wanted (former [Boston College](2025-10-03_bchockey_mens) forward Ryan Leonard, who I saw a few times during his college career).

As the building is named for Capital One (a bank), there's a discount on merch if you use a Capital One card, and since I've already made the "what's in your wallet" reference before...of course, you still have to deal with the arena upcharges, but based on price hikes for jerseys this year, you actually still come out "ahead". However, be forewarned that said discount does NOT apply to the practice facility store (i.e. no discounts on the Authentic Pros), and DC (and Virginia for that matter) have sales tax on clothing.

One last thing to mention: A table on the upper concourse run by MeiGray (a company specializing in game-used sports stuff) offers the chance to buy pucks used during warm-ups. They are, sadly, not eligible for the Capital One discount, and I've heard that prices can vary WILDLY on a game-to-game basis (aka "don't ask how much they cost for this particular game"), but at least in the Capitals' case, they are a VERY special memento for each individual game, as they feature the date of the game and both team's logos - like this:

![A photo of a warm-up puck](/images/2026/1011-warmuppuck.jpeg)

Many teams in the NHL also sell warm up pucks, but they all do it a bit differently - and apparently this design is not "constant" throughout the league. For example, I once got a warm up puck at a Bruins-Devils game in Newark at the team store kiosk in the main concourse's large "plaza" area; it looked similar to this one, but I've heard the team no longer uses these types of warm up pucks...
