I've mentioned that hockey is probably my favorite winter sport to watch, but the National Hockey League is currently the only major sports league that has yet to feature on this blog. Well, that changes today, because I'm writing about the NHL for the first time...ever!

I'm not sure how many NHL games I've been to vs. MLB, but the last NHL game I went to was a preseason game in October 2024 - JUST before I started writing about things that aren't baseball or college hockey. It was the Rangers and Devils at the [Prudential Center](2025-08-27_nysirens) in Newark, and while it did feature almost none of either team's NHL squads as the main teams were in Europe (though there was one Nikita Zadorov), the Devils had a certain famous name in net: Jeremy Brodeur, son of franchise legend Martin Brodeur (whom I actually got to see at least three times).

I'm not going to discuss my NHL allegiances here, but as a reminder: I live in New Jersey - to be more specific, North Jersey[^1]. In my experience, this area seems rather split between two major hockey teams: the New Jersey Devils and the New York Rangers; while most folks I've met are Devils fans (such as many of the people I went to high school with), I do feel like I know a fair amount of Ranger fans from the area. Whether that be due to the Rangers having been around 56 years longer than the Devils or Christians who cannot get behind supporting a team named the "Devils" I don't know, but one thing I do know: the fans of each team HATE the other team.

HOWEVER...

Despite them being the "closer" team, the thing that prevents me from going to more Devils games (outside of ticket prices): Prudential Center's location. While downtown Newark isn't necessarily the worst place in the world, speaking from experience...it's not exactly "great". Yes, there are a number of parking lots very close to the arena, but given that they're arena lots, they're not exactly "cheap" for events; my dad was a monthly pass holder in one of the garages pre-COVID (as his office was a few blocks away), and he'd have to move his car prior to events to avoid having to pay event rates. In addition, while there is moderately convenient train service to the arena (as Newark Penn Station is connected via an elevated walkway), getting there from where I live is fairly awkward due to the "set up" of the city's trains; the station nearest me does not have direct access to Newark Penn, so I'd need to take a "connecting" train to get from Newark Broad Street to Newark Penn Station (and speaking from personal experience: both Newark Penn Station and Newark Broad Street are not the kind of places you want to be at for long periods of time - even in broad daylight)[^2].

The Rangers, on the other hand, are somehow better located despite being further away. Sure, driving into New York City sucks and parking is even worse, but unlike the Devils, trains are WAY more convenient, as their arena sits atop New York Penn Station - America's busiest train hub with direct connection to my station. Yes, it does feature a few...interesting sights (especially if you're there at 2 AM - something that I've done once before), but with just how much more "normal" people there are (because of Amtrak AND LIRR), it doesn't feel as "bad" as either Newark station. Also, I feel like I can at least "get behind" the Rangers WAY more than I ever could with the [Yankees](2026-06-04_nyyankees) (some of that being the "James Dolan pity effect"), so going to a game at Madison Square Garden - the self proclaimed "World's Most Famous Arena" - is definitely something I felt I'd be interested in. Having said that...MSG is not easy to get into.

The Rangers have had a few down years recently, but it feels like even in those years, tickets on the secondary market STILL cost over $100[^3]. This game - being a preseason game - still had seats that felt WAY overpriced ($2K to watch a bunch of maybes and prospects)...but also some that were not so bad - and a couple of days before the game, THIS was available for just over 50 bucks (and at least 20% of that was fees):

Anyway, I realize I haven't actually gotten into the team at hand, but I also don't know how much that's necessary given their status. The big thing you need to know about the Rangers (outside of them being an "Original Six" team) is 1994 - the year they won a Stanley Cup that broke a 54-year drought that THEY WILL NOT SHUT UP ABOUT (much to the chagrin of the Vancouver Canucks in more than one way). One thing you may not have known about the team (or, to be more specific, their home): it's not the original Madison Square Garden.

Madison Square Garden was opened in 1879 and named after a nearby public park[^4], but was apparently so dangerous that it was closed about ten years later. Its replacement - also named Madison Square Garden and built in the same area as the original arena - opened in 1890, but was closed in 1925 - one year before the Rangers debuted. A THIRD Madison Square Garden opened in 1925 and was the original home of the Rangers - despite being NOWHERE close to Madison Square. This MSG lasted until 1968, when the fourth (and current) Madison Square Garden was opened in the heart of Midtown Manhattan (and by virtue of it being nearly 60 years old, it holds the title of "longest lasting Madison Square Garden").

I mentioned earlier that the arena is on top of Penn Station; his has been fairly controversial since the beginning, as it replaced an ornate station building with, in essence, brutalist concourses. In an attempt to revive the "vibe", Penn Station has expanded itself into a nearby building which was largely vacant prior to the renovations, but that's getting WAY off topic (and also it's hard to "use" it if you're an NJ Transit customer like me), so let's get back to the arena.

Given its location in the heart of Manhattan, it should come as no surprise that it's one of New York's most prestigious concert venues in addition to being the home of the Rangers and Knicks. In fact, while this is the first time I've been to MSG for a sporting event, it's not my first visit to the place: I came here for a concert in 2019 (for a band which has since been "cancelled by association" - don't ask), and I also did a tour of the place once (which was by and large kinda "okay at best" - though I did get to hear Michael Buble doing vocal-warm ups as well as see the Knicks locker room).

One thing about MSG: the building received a MASSIVE "transformation" during the 2010s, and as a side effect of this transformation, sightlines from some sections are obscured. Case in point: when I was poking around the various sections while seeking tickets (i.e. the days before finding my seat), I found that the highest sections had no view of the scoreboard and, and that certain rows in the "sideline" sections had what is known as a "bridge" obscuring views (though there's screens - something [Citi Field](2026-05-01_nymets) has for the back rows of some of its sections).

### MERCH



As this is the first time I'm covering the National Hockey League on this blog, there is one other thing I do need to discuss: Retail Jersey Tiers!

While retail hockey jerseys have gone through a slew of "systems" over the years, the current system was, more or less, adopted in 2017 when Adidas became the league's jersey manufacturer. in 2024, a certain company which shall remain nameless "took over", but they largely kept the same system Adidas was using before then. 

1. Breakaway: These are basically the "replica" level for the NHL, and they've actually been manufactured by the same company (i.e. the faceless company now responsible for ALL NHL jerseys) since 2017. These are the least expensive, but they're also not that great: the material feels a bit rough IMO, and the graphics are a bit lacking (usually flat with an appearance of being screen printed). It should be noted, however, that for a while, replica hockey jerseys did not have stitched-on logos (namely in the CCM and Reebok eras[^8]), and the ones in the current system do (or at least the front logos are stitched).
2. Premium: This jersey is a step up from the replica: all logos are stitched (and even feature a bit of texture), and the jersey has a "fight strap" (which is meant to keep the jersey from being ripped off of a player in a fight). From 2017 until 2024, however, this was called an "authentic" jersey despite not actually being what players wore during games (as well as the fact that they were made in Indonesia), but it was the closest fans could get to what the pros actually wore, as those jerseys were largely unavailable to the public (i.e. not sold in stores).
3. Authentic Pro: This is what the players wear. They're made in Canada and are NOTABLY different from the "Authentic" jersey...but they're also not cheap: I believe prices for these start around $400, and as someone who prefers physical retail over online retail, they're difficult to find in stores that aren't official team stores.

Having said that, I will admit that I've found MOST of the non-[Boston Bruins](2026-03-21_hechampionship2024) NHL stuff I've gotten - i.e. jerseys and pucks (and the occasional hat and/or mini stick, though I've been trying to cut down on those recently) - at various Dick's Sporting Goods plus the NHL Store a few blocks from MSG. Coincidentally, I actually got a Rangers jersey here around the time of the 2024 switchover (as they were on sale then). 

As you'd expect for a major arena, there are several merchandise areas dotted around the concourses. What I find interesting about MSG is that the main store - i.e. the one that is open outside of events - is fully inside the arena's "secure" area, so if you're trying to visit it during non-event times (which I've done in the past), you still have to go through security.

(Finally, I feel like I should mention Gerry Cosby - a old local sports shop across the street - here; I got a few things from them back in the day, but they seem to have closed/retired.)
