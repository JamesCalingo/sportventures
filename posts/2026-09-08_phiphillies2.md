I've mentioned the possibility (or rather, unfortunate likelihood) of an [MLB lockout](2026-05-02_1994ws) that could have MASSIVE impacts not just in 2027, but beyond as well. The last thing I've heard is that things are very much NOT going well, and some owners are prepared to lose AN ENTIRE SEASON of baseball if necessary. With that in mind, I also have a different goal in mind - one I kinda alluded to at the [last MLB game I was at two days ago](2026-09-06_nymets2).

Y'see, the MLB Ballpark app has this feature where it tracks which stadiums/teams you've seen based on which games you attend - including a feature to manually add games you went to in the past (such as games that occurred prior to the app's debut in 2012). Heading into this game, I've seen 18 of 30 MLB teams, and my goal is to get to 30/30 by 2030 (lockout be damned). To this end, I had several games I was tracking on SeatGeek for potential visits, but wasn't able to make it to most of them for various reasons[^1].

This game is one of those contests I was tracking - a National League East showdown between the Phillies and Atlanta Braves with postseason implications on Labor Day. While tickets weren't SUPER expensive despite the two teams, I was a bit hesitant on committing to one - and then I got an email: as part of a promotion involving the team's All Stars, the Phillies had a flash sale with special ticket prices for select games - and this was one of the games[^2]. Sure, the only tickets available were in the 400 section, but SeatGeek's least expensive tickets were still double what I ended up paying for this seat during the sale (and were unlikely to get any "better"). Funnily enough, I also saw this game go on sale about a week or two before it happened, but the prices for seats arguably WORSE than what I got were HIGHER than what I paid (and as a reminder: this was all "firsthand" pricing). I won't say that this was "merely" an upper deck seat, as I honestly LOVE this view you get of the game from up top (and it includes views of the Philadelphia skyline, which you don't really get at the lower levels):

![An image of Citizens Bank Park](/images/2026/0907-fullfield.jpeg)

### MERCH

I didn't think I'd be doing a merch section in this post, but here we are!

First, I probably should've mentioned this as more than just a footnote last time, but the Phillies are a bit different from other teams in that they don't publish a "magazine". Instead, they have these scorecards with a single article plus the standard "how to score a baseball" guide for "programs", which are much more compact (and less expensive than a regular program). At [the last Phillies game I was at](2026-06-08_phiphillies), I got that game's card plus ones for the games before it, and at this game, I got the scorecards for the games between my last trip and now. As this game's scorecard is the last card for this season, I (think I) now have a full set of Phillies scorecards for the 2026 regular season - NONE of which have a game score recorded in them.

Second, there was a deal on merchandise associated with the Phillies' City Connect identity, where if you got a jersey, hat, and shirt from the team's City Connect collection, it was 40% off (City Connect being a Nike program wherein MOST MLB teams have a special jersey meant to harken to the city they play in)[^3]. I should've mentioned this when I was at Citi Field [two days ago](2026-09-06_nymets2), but the Mets also had a 40% off deal on their City Connect jerseys. What that means I'm not fully certain, but I do know that teams tend to cycle through City Connects on a three year schedule (the Mets and Phillies both debuted their current City Connect uniforms in 2024), so it's very possible that one or both teams are planning on releasing new City Connects soon (again, whether or not they hit the field next year is questionable right now).

Finally, I decided to visit the Eagles team store at Lincoln Financial Field across the street (as I had arrived early to ensure that the parking I wanted was available). While I've said that I'm not interested in football things, there are two Eagles players whose jerseys I'm mildly interested in due to a connection I share with them (which also happens to involve the day the Eagles won that Super Bowl over the Chiefs). Alas, I'm not a fan of replica football jerseys as the name and number are usually not that great, and while they did have an Authentic jersey for one of the players, I'm not the biggest fan of the "style" of the jersey they had (it's a throwback), plus it wasn't super discounted like the Sauce Gardner jerseys at [MetLife](2026-08-15_nyjets). However, they did have discounted jerseys for a player the team traded this past offseason (including "Authentics" - but those were only available in 3XL and 4XL...).

### Your own personal Jesús _(hey-soos)_

This game ended up being a pitchers' duel - a game where the pitchers for both teams excel and hits and runs are hard to come by. Exactly ONE mistake was made by either side's pitchers, and it was the game's lone relief pitcher who pitched for the Braves - star Phillies hitter Kyle Schwarber turned a misplaced fastball late in the game into one of his patented home runs (commonly known as "Schwarbombs").

That Schwarbomb ended up being enough for Phillies starter Jesús Luzardo, who was absolutely dominant - pitching a complete game shutout (i.e. he was the only Phillies pitcher and gave up zero runs). In the modern era of MLB where pitchers are HEAVILY regulated (i.e. they're pulled after like 70 pitches and/or four innings), it's INSANELY rare for a starting pitcher to even be allowed close to finishing the game by himself - and it seems the crowd knew it as well, as the place LIT UP when he came out to pitch the ninth inning.

Assuming this is the final MLB game I get to see in person before 2028, at least I had a SUPER good game to end this year on (and it was under two hours too!).
